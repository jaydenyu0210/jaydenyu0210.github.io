PROJECT OVERVIEW

wellbeingABC — Coaching Marketplace

wellbeingABC is a coaching marketplace in English and Chinese. Anyone can take a self-assessment and book a session without an account. Coaches list what they offer, keep a calendar, chat and get paid.

I did all of it over seven months: research, screens, both languages, the build. Some calls were wrong and I undid them. Those are the ones worth reading.

Sole designer, researcher and builder

7 months

Designed and built solo, and AI-paired throughout with Claude Code.

The Problem

It is eleven at night. A woman is on her phone, trying to work out why the last few months felt heavy. She is not in crisis. She is not ready to book a therapist.

She wants a name for what she is feeling. The first site she opens asks for her email before it tells her anything. She closes it.

She also reads English slowly. Translating the menu does nothing for her. She still has to answer a page of questions about her own mood in a second language. The hard part was never the navigation.

What I Did to Learn

No budget and no team, so I asked people directly. Nine gave me half an hour each: five who had thought about hiring a coach, four who coach for a living. Four of the talks ran in Chinese. I asked what they had tried, where they stopped, and what worried them about paying.

Three Things That Changed My Mind

I came in with a signup screen and two separate apps. Both went.

01

Signing up costs more than paying

I showed a rough version in those same calls. Four of the five buyers stopped at the account screen. Not one of them stopped at the price.

02

Both sides want the same four things

I asked coaches what they do all day. They write, they take assessments, they chat, they buy from each other. That is the same list the clients gave me.

03

The worry sits after payment

Nobody doubted the card would go through. They worried about the hour afterwards. Buyers feared a no-show. Coaches feared an argument about whether it happened.

Three Calls and What They Cost

There was nobody to lose an argument to. So I wrote the price next to each call and lived with it.

Guests are customers, not visitors

01

You can buy here without an account. A guest books with a name, an email and a confirmation number. Her result stays on her phone, and moves into an account only if she makes one. The cost: a private window loses it, and I chose that over holding mood scores for strangers.

The buyer releases the money, not the coach

02

Sessions happen off the site, so nothing I can see proves one took place. The coach does not get the money until the buyer says the session happened. If she never clicks, he is never paid. There is no reminder and no deadline, because I never built one.

I deleted the language question three times over

03

Coaches picked their language at signup, again on the profile, again on every listing. Three of the four wrote in Chinese and then ticked English anyway. I removed all three questions and read the language off what they had already written. The cost: a coach who works in both can no longer say so.

01 Land Opens in the language you read
02 Assess One question at a time, no account
03 Result Plain words, kept on your own phone
04 Browse Coaches who work in your language
05 Book Pay as a guest, name and email
06 Hold The money stops here and waits
07 Release You say it happened, the coach is paid

The marked step is where the money waits. Nothing moves until the buyer says the session happened.

What Broke in Front of People

Two rounds, eight people, on their own phones. Five in the first round and three new people in the second, so nobody repeated a task. Five buyers and three coaches in all.

A control that forgot every time

01

Four of the five in round one set their assessment result to private. It never stuck. Not once. I fixed the rule so it holds, then cut the sharing choices from four to three, because nobody could explain the fourth.

People backed out at the sign-up

02

Three of the five buyers thought signing up would wipe the answers they had just given. Two quit right there. Now the screen says the result is kept and lands in the new account. Both buyers in round two went straight through.

An email scanner released the money

03

One buyer's work email opened the release link before she did. The money moved for a session nobody had confirmed. Now the link opens a page with a button on it. A person has to press it.

Where It Landed

8 people Testers across two rounds, on their own phones
3 of 5 Round-one buyers who feared signing up would erase their answers
2 of 2 New buyers in round two who booked with no help

It shipped in both languages, down to every assessment question. A guest can reach a booked session with no account. Coaches publish, schedule, chat and withdraw what they earn. Three things are not done. The privacy control is still English only. The WeChat build never launched, and the old name still shows in the app.

It has not had enough traffic to tell you anything. Next I would write the cancellation rules, because right now they are a promise, not a policy. Then I would hand the written Chinese to a native reader, since writing it and speaking it are different jobs. Working alone, nothing got blocked. Nothing got challenged either.

What I Took Away

  • A control that fails quietly is worse than one that was never there.
  • If someone has already told you by writing it, asking again is the bug.
